ATLAS-Canada

The Canadian ATLAS Tier-1 Data Centre

The Tier-1 Centre at SFU processes data recorded by the ATLAS experiment, so researchers can study particles like the Higgs boson.

It is one of ten ATLAS Tier-1 centres in the world, and is run by a consortium of ten Canadian institutions led by SFU and TRIUMF: the ATLAS-Canada collaboration, more than 150 researchers working on every part of the experiment.

ATLAS Tier-1 Data Centre website →

What the Tier-1 Centre does

  • Processing. Computing capacity to process LHC collisions recorded by ATLAS.
  • Storage. Keeps raw and derived datasets from the experiment.
  • Simulation and analysis. Runs simulations and physics analysis for researchers.

History

  1. 2007–2018The Tier-1 Centre is based at TRIUMF in Vancouver, created with support from the Canada Foundation for Innovation (CFI) and the B.C. Knowledge Development Fund.
  2. 2018As Canada consolidates research computing into a few national centres, the Tier-1 moves to the SFU data centre that also hosts the national supercomputer.
  3. 2026A new $5.3 million CFI award expands the centre for the High-Luminosity LHC, when ATLAS will record far more data.

How it fits together

The Worldwide LHC Computing Grid

No single computer centre could handle all the LHC's data. CERN coordinates a worldwide network of computing centres, linked by grid software so that they act as one large system.

  1. Tier-0

    CERN

    ATLAS records the collisions, and the data are sent out across the grid.

  2. Tier-1

    SFU, Burnaby

    One of ten ATLAS Tier-1 centres. Stores and processes ATLAS data for Canada and the world.

  3. Researchers

    Physicists worldwide

    Run simulations and analyses on the grid, including the SFU group's Higgs and new-physics searches.

Cedar Supercomputing Centre

Fir: Canada's fastest academic supercomputer

Fir became fully operational at SFU in September 2025, replacing the Cedar supercomputer.

It is the largest general-purpose research supercomputer in Canada and part of the national platform run with the Digital Research Alliance of Canada. When it launched it ranked 78th on the TOP500 list of the world's supercomputers, the only Canadian system in the top 100.

The Cedar Supercomputing Centre serves more than 17,000 researchers, about 70% of them outside B.C., in fields from particle physics to genomics and artificial intelligence. It runs on B.C.'s clean energy.
