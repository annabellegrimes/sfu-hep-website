Do research at the frontier of physics

Analyze collisions from the world's largest particle accelerator, hunt for neutrinos from deep space, or help build a telescope on the floor of the Pacific. We welcome graduate and undergraduate students who want to try.

Choose your experiment

Students join one of three experiments, each in one of the most extreme places on Earth. Most combine physics analysis with hands-on work on the detector.

At the Large Hadron Collider

ATLAS

Measure the Higgs boson and search for new particles with machine learning, and assemble and test petals for the new ATLAS tracker with the SFU and TRIUMF team.

Research areas for students →
At the South Pole

IceCube

Track down the sources of cosmic neutrinos, measure how neutrinos change type as they cross the Earth, and search the data for dark matter, in a collaboration of more than 450 people in 14 countries.

Research areas for students →
In the deep Pacific

P‑ONE

A young experiment that students help shape: build calibration hardware, track the detector lines as they sway in the currents, and analyze the very first data.

Research areas for students →

What you'll learn

Whichever experiment you join, you'll pick up skills that are in demand in physics and well beyond it.

Data analysis

Find a rare signal in a mountain of data, with statistics and machine learning.

Detector hardware

Build and test real detector parts, from tracker petals to deep-sea instruments.

Computing at scale

Run analyses over petabytes on the ATLAS Tier-1 centre and national supercomputers.

Global teamwork

Work in international collaborations and present your results to them.

From first year

Undergraduate research

  1. First year: Adopt-a-Physicist

    First-year physics students can be "adopted" by a research group: join group meetings and gradually take part in the research. About Adopt-a-Physicist

  2. Summer research awards

    Undergraduate Student Research Awards (USRA) fund summer research. Canadian citizens and permanent residents apply for the NSERC award; international students for the SFU VPR Sciences award. About USRA at SFU Physics

  3. Ask about other positions

    Summer and co-op research positions are sometimes available. Email a faculty member to ask.
