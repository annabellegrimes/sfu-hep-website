Research

The group works on two experiments: ATLAS at CERN's Large Hadron Collider, and P-ONE, a neutrino telescope in the Pacific Ocean.

ATLAS at the Large Hadron Collider

ATLAS is one of the two general-purpose detectors at the LHC. SFU physicists study the properties of the Higgs boson, search for new long-lived particles and other signs of physics beyond the Standard Model, and work on detector calibration and track reconstruction.

The new ATLAS Inner Tracker (ITk)

For the High-Luminosity LHC upgrade, ATLAS is replacing its inner tracking detector. The SFU and TRIUMF team built the first tracker "petal" ready for installation and plans to make about 100 of the 400 petals being produced worldwide.

P-ONE: the Pacific Ocean Neutrino Experiment

P-ONE is a planned neutrino telescope anchored to the deep Pacific seafloor off Vancouver Island. Mooring lines about one kilometre long carry optical modules that detect the faint Cherenkov light made when neutrinos interact in the water. SFU leads the project's science, with Professor Matthias Danninger as scientific spokesperson, and built calibration detectors and light emitters that measure the optical properties of seawater.

The first line was assembled at TRIUMF and wet-tested in June 2026, and is planned for deployment about 2,700 metres down, connected to Ocean Networks Canada's NEPTUNE observatory. The next goal is a demonstrator of four or five lines. Read the SFU News story.
